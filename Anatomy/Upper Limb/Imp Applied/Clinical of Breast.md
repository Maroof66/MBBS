# Clinical Anatomy of Breast

### 1. Carcinoma breast

* **Common site:** upper and outer quadrant.
* Carcinoma usually arises from the **epithelium of large lactiferous ducts**.
* **Sentinel lymph node** = first lymph node draining the tumour-bearing area.

### 2. Changes due to local infiltration

* **Cooper's ligaments** → infiltration + contraction → **fixation and puckering/retraction of skin**.
* **Lactiferous ducts** → fibrosis → **retraction of nipple**.
* **Superficial lymphatics** → obstruction → skin oedema → **peau d'orange appearance**.

### 3. Spread of carcinoma

* Mainly through **lymphatics → axillary lymph nodes**.
* Communication of superficial lymphatics across midline → **spread to opposite breast**.
* Communication with abdominal lymphatics → **liver/pelvic spread**; ovarian metastasis may produce **Krukenberg tumour**.
* Venous spread → **vertebrae and brain**.

### 4. Diagnosis

* **Breast self-examination** → detects lump, nipple retraction/discharge, skin changes and axillary nodes.
* **Mammography** → detects suspicious mass.
* **FNAC** → rapid diagnosis of breast lesions.

### 5. Surgical importance

* Breast incisions are generally made **radially** to avoid cutting lactiferous ducts.
* **Mastectomy** = partial/complete surgical removal of breast.
* **Radical mastectomy** = removal of breast + pectoral muscles + axillary lymph nodes.

# Reasonings 

## Clinical Anatomy of Breast — High-Yield Reasoning

### 1. Carcinoma breast

* **Common site:** upper and outer quadrant.
* **Sentinel lymph node:** first lymph node draining the tumour-bearing area.
* Carcinoma commonly spreads through **lymphatics**, especially to **axillary nodes**.

### 2. Why are breast incisions made radially?

**Incisions → made radially**

* Lactiferous ducts run towards the nipple.
* Radial incision therefore minimizes damage to the ducts.

### 3. Why does the breast become fixed?

**Cancer → infiltrates suspensory ligaments of Cooper → fibrosis/shortening → breast becomes fixed.**

### 4. Why does skin become puckered/retracted?

**Cancer → infiltration of Cooper's ligaments → contraction → pulling of skin → puckering/retraction.**

### 5. Why is the nipple retracted?

**Cancer → infiltration of lactiferous ducts → fibrosis + shortening → nipple retraction.**

⭐ **Retracted nipple = important sign of breast carcinoma.**

### 6. Peau d'orange appearance

**Cancer cells → obstruct superficial lymphatics → lymphatic drainage impaired → oedema of skin → peau d'orange appearance.**

Think:

**Lymphatic obstruction → skin oedema → orange-peel appearance**

### 7. Spread to opposite breast

* Superficial lymphatics of the two breasts **communicate across the midline**.
* Therefore, carcinoma can spread from one breast to the opposite breast.

### 8. Spread to abdomen/liver

* Lymphatics of the lower breast communicate with **subdiaphragmatic and subperitoneal lymphatic plexuses**.
* Therefore, carcinoma may spread to abdominal structures, including the **liver**.

### 9. Spread to ovary

* Tumour cells may reach the ovary and produce a **secondary ovarian tumour (Krukenberg tumour)**.

### 10. Spread through veins

**Breast → venous channels → vertebral venous plexus → vertebrae / brain**

This explains distant metastasis through venous pathways.

## Other clinical points

### Diagnosis

* **Mammography** → detects suspicious breast masses.
* **Fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC)** → rapid method for diagnosis of breast lesions.
* **Self-examination** → looks for:

  * Breast asymmetry
  * Skin/nipple changes
  * Nipple retraction
  * Nipple discharge
  * Breast lump
  * Axillary lymph-node enlargement

### Treatment

* **Mastectomy:** surgical removal of part or whole breast.
* **Radical mastectomy:** removal of breast + underlying pectoral muscles + axillary lymph nodes.
