*“Injury to median nerve at or above elbow.”*
# MEDIAN NERVE — APPLIED ANATOMY

### Introduction

* Injury of median nerve **above or at the elbow** may occur in **supracondylar fracture of humerus**.
* Produces **motor, sensory, vasomotor and trophic changes**.

### Clinical Features

**1. Paralysis of long flexors**

* **Flexor pollicis longus** → unable to flex terminal phalanx of thumb.
* **Lateral half of flexor digitorum profundus** → inability to flex terminal phalanx of index and middle fingers.
* Tested by holding proximal phalanx firmly and asking patient to flex terminal phalanx.

**2. Loss of pronation**

* Pronator teres and pronator quadratus paralysed.
* Forearm therefore tends to remain in **supination**.

**3. Weak wrist flexion**

* **Flexor carpi radialis** paralysed.
* Wrist flexion becomes weak.
* Hand deviates/adducts toward ulnar side during attempted flexion.

**4. Pointing index / Hand of benediction ⭐**

* Paralysis of long flexors of index and middle fingers.
* During making a fist:

  * Index finger remains extended → **pointing index**.
  * If both index and middle fingers remain extended → **hand of benediction**.

**5. Ape thumb / Monkey thumb ⭐**

* Paralysis of thenar muscles.
* Loss of effective **opposition of thumb**.
* Thumb lies in the same plane as the fingers → **ape thumb deformity**.

**6. Sensory loss**

* Loss of sensation over **lateral 3½ digits** and corresponding nail beds.

**7. Vasomotor & trophic changes**

* Skin over lateral 3½ digits becomes:

  * **Warm**
  * **Dry**
  * **Scaly**
* Nails may become **cracked**.

**8. Pen test**

* Tests **abductor pollicis brevis**.
* In median nerve injury → weakness/paralysis of abduction of thumb.

### ⭐ Exam summary

| Lesion                                 | Main finding                         |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| FPL + lateral FDP paralysis            | Loss of terminal phalanx flexion     |
| Pronator paralysis                     | Supinated forearm                    |
| FCR paralysis                          | Weak wrist flexion                   |
| Long flexors of index/middle paralysed | Pointing index / hand of benediction |
| Thenar paralysis                       | Ape thumb                            |
| Sensory fibres affected                | Loss over lateral 3½ digits          |
| Autonomic fibres affected              | Warm, dry, scaly skin                |
| APB testing                            | Pen test                             |

### 🔥 The 5 things you absolutely shouldn't miss

**Supination + pointing index/benediction + ape thumb + sensory loss lateral 3½ + pen test.**

One important distinction for exams: **“hand of benediction” describes the posture when the patient attempts to make a fist with a high median nerve lesion**; it is different from the **“ape thumb”** deformity, which is due to thenar paralysis.
