## Introduction
 - Thick, bulky, fan-shaped muscle of Pectoral Region
## Origin
 - Clavicular head: 
   - Anterior surface of medial two-thirds of clavicle
 - Sternocostal head:
   - Lateral half of anterior surface of manubrium and sternum up to 6th costal cartilage
   - 2nd to 6th costal cartilages, sternal end of 6th rib
   - Aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle of abdomen
## Insertion
- It is inserted by a bilaminar tendon on the lateral lip of the bicipital groove in form of U-shaped bilaminar tendon
- The two laminae are continuous with each other inferiorly
  - Anterior Lamina:
    - thicker and shorter
    - receives superficial clavicular and deep manubrial fibres
  - Posterior Lamina: 
    - thinner and longer
    - gets costal, sternal and aponeurotic fibres
## Nerve Supply 
 - Medial and lateral pectoral nerves (Composite/Hybrid muscle)
## Blood Supply
- Pectoral branch: Main supply from the thoracoacromial artery.
- Perforating branches: Blood flow from the internal thoracic (mammary) artery via intercostal spaces.
- Superior thoracic artery: Minor direct contribution.
## Action
  - As a whole the muscle: Adduction and medial rotation of the shoulder joint (arm)
  - Clavicular part: Flexion of the arm
  - Sternocostal part: Extension of flexed arm against resistance
## Clinical
  - Clinical Testing of Pectoralis Major

    1. **Clavicular head**

        * Flex the arm to **90°** → clavicular head becomes prominent.
        * Lifting a heavy object also makes the **clavicular part** prominent.

    2. **Sternocostal head**

        * Extend the flexed arm **against resistance** → sternocostal head becomes prominent.
        * Alternatively, abduct the arm to **60°** and touch the **opposite hip**.

    3. **Whole muscle**

        * Pressing the **fists against each other** makes the whole pectoralis major prominent.

### 🔥 Easy recall

**Clavicular → Flexion / Lift**
**Sternocostal → Extension / Opposite hip**
**Whole → Fists together**
