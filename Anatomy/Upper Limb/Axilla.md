## Introduction
 - Axilla = armpit 
 - pyramidal space situated between the upper part of the arm and the chest wall
 - resembles a four-sided pyramid
 - disposed obliquely in such a way that the apex is directed upwards and medially towards the root of the neck, and the base is directed downwards
## Boundaries
### Apex
   * directed upwards and medially towards the root of the neck
   * truncated (not pointed)
   * corresponds to a triangular, oblique passage called the cervicoaxillary canal
   *  This canal is bounded:
      *  Anteriorly: by the posterior surface of clavicle.
      *  Posteriorly,: by the superior border of the scapula and medial aspect of coracoid process.
      *  Medially: by the outer border of the first rib.
   *  This canal gives passage to the: 
      *  axillary artery 
      *  axillary vein 
      *  brachial plexus
### Base
* directed downwards, convex upwards.
* formed by skin, superficial & axillary fascia
* extends from anterior to posterior axillary folds
* medially: continuous with the chest wall 
* laterally: with the medial side of the arm
### Anterior wall
1. Pectoralis major in front 
2. Clavipectoral fascia
3. Pectoralis minor.
### Posterior Wall
1. Subscapularis above
2. Teres major
3. Latissimus dorsi below
### Medial Wall
convex laterally
1. Upper four ribs with thier intercostal muscles
2. Upper part of the serratous anterior
### Lateral Wall
1. Upper part of the shaft of the humerus in the region of the bicipital groove
2. Coracobrachialis and short head of biceps brachii
## Axillary Folds
*  Axillary folds are folds of skin within which a margin of muscles is enclosed 
   *  Anterior axillary fold: covers the lateral edge of pectoralis major muscle 
   *  Posterior axillary fold: covers the latissimus dorsi and teres major muscle
## Contents of Axilla
   1. Axillary artery and its branches
   2. Axillary vein and its tributaries
   3. Axillary lymph nodes
   4. Infraclavicular part of the brachial plexus
   5. Long thoracic nerves 
   6. Intercostobrachial nerves
   7. Axillary tail of mammary gland
   8. Axillary fat and areolar tissue
## Applied
 * The axilla has abundant axillary hair. Infection of the hair follicles and sebaceous glands gives rise to boils, which are common in this area.
