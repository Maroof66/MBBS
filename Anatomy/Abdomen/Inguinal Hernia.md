# Inguinal Hernia 

## Definition

**Protrusion of abdominal contents into the inguinal canal** is called an **inguinal hernia**.

There are two types:

1. **Indirect inguinal hernia**
2. **Direct inguinal hernia**

---

## 1. Indirect Inguinal Hernia ⭐

**Definition:** Abdominal contents enter the inguinal canal through the **deep inguinal ring**.

### Types

* **Congenital:** Due to persistent **processus vaginalis**, maintaining communication between peritoneal cavity and tunica vaginalis.
* **Acquired:** Due to increased intra-abdominal pressure, e.g. weightlifting.

### Features

* Common in **children and young adults**
* Hernial sac lies **lateral to inferior epigastric artery**
* **More common** than direct hernia
* More common in **males**

### Coverings — inside → outside

1. Parietal peritoneum + extraperitoneal connective tissue
2. Internal spermatic fascia
3. Cremasteric fascia
4. External spermatic fascia
5. Skin

---

## 2. Direct Inguinal Hernia ⭐

**Definition:** Abdominal contents enter the inguinal canal through its **posterior wall**.

### Features

* More common in **elderly** due to weakness of abdominal wall
* Hernial sac lies **medial to inferior epigastric artery**
* Occurs through **Hesselbach's triangle**
* More common in **females**

### Types

**A. Lateral direct hernia**

* Through lateral part of Hesselbach's triangle
* Lateral to medial umbilical ligament

**Coverings:**

1. Parietal peritoneum + extraperitoneal tissue
2. Fascia transversalis
3. Cremasteric fascia
4. External spermatic fascia
5. Skin

**B. Medial direct hernia**

* Through medial part of Hesselbach's triangle
* Medial to medial umbilical ligament

**Coverings:**

1. Parietal peritoneum + extraperitoneal tissue
2. Fascia transversalis
3. Conjoint tendon
4. External spermatic fascia
5. Skin

---

## Key Difference

| Feature                    | Indirect                                    | Direct                |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Entry                      | **Deep inguinal ring**                      | **Posterior wall**    |
| Inferior epigastric artery | **Lateral**                                 | **Medial**            |
| Site                       | Deep ring → canal                           | Hesselbach's triangle |
| Age                        | Children/young adults                       | Elderly               |
| Cause                      | Persistent processus vaginalis / ↑ pressure | Weak abdominal wall   |
| Frequency                  | More common                                 | Less common           |

---

## Complications

1. **Irreducibility** — hernia cannot return to abdominal cavity.
2. **Obstruction** — intestinal contents cannot pass through the herniated loop.
3. **Strangulation** — arterial supply is compromised → **necrosis**.

## Treatment

* Depends on cause, symptoms and complications.
* **Herniorrhaphy:** surgical repair of the hernia/weak abdominal wall.
* **Ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerves** may be damaged during surgery.
