# FIBROUS JOINTS

### Definition

* In **fibrous joints**, bones are united by **fibrous connective tissue**.
* They are generally **immovable or permit slight movement**.
* They are classified into **three types**:

### 1. Sutures ⭐

* Present **only in the skull**.
* Adjacent bones are separated by a layer of **fibrous connective tissue**.
* Usually **immovable**.
* With advancing age, the sutures may undergo **synostosis** and become obliterated.

**Types according to shape of bony margins:**

* **Plane** → internasal suture
* **Serrate** → sagittal suture
* **Squamous** → temporoparietal suture
* **Denticulate** → lambdoid suture
* **Schindylesis** → rostrum of sphenoid with upper border of vomer

**Fontanelles:**

* Widened, membrane-filled areas between cranial bones in the **newborn skull**.
* Permit **moulding during childbirth** and allow expansion of the growing brain.
* Become ossified by about **18 months**.

### 2. Syndesmosis ⭐

* Fibrous union between bones by:

  * **Interosseous ligament** → inferior tibiofibular joint
  * **Interosseous membrane** → between bones where applicable
  * **Interosseous talocalcaneal ligament**
* Permits a **small degree of movement**.

### 3. Gomphosis

* **Peg-and-socket type** of fibrous joint between a **tooth and its alveolar socket**.
* The tooth is attached to the alveolar bone by the **periodontal ligament**.
* Essentially an articulation between the tooth and its socket.

### 🧠 Quick recall

**Fibrous joints → SSG**

**S**utures → skull → immovable
**S**yndesmosis → ligament/membrane → slight movement
**G**omphosis → tooth + socket → periodontal ligament
