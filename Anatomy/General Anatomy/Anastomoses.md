# ANASTOMOSES
### Definition

* **Anastomosis** is a communication between neighbouring **precapillary or postcapillary vessels**.
* Blood flow through an anastomosis is called **collateral circulation**.

## Types of Anastomoses ⭐

### A. Arterial Anastomosis

Communication between arteries or their branches.

**1. Actual arterial anastomosis**

* Arteries communicate by **end-to-end or direct communication**.
* Examples:

  * **Palmar arches**
  * Plantar arch
  * Circle of Willis
  * Intestinal arterial arcades
  * Labial branches of facial artery

**2. Potential arterial anastomosis**

* Communication occurs between **terminal arterioles**.
* These vessels can dilate gradually to establish collateral circulation.
* Therefore, they may **not compensate adequately after sudden arterial occlusion**.
* **Example:** Coronary arteries.

---

### B. Venous Anastomosis

* Communication between neighbouring veins or their tributaries.
* **Examples:**

  * Dorsal venous arch of hand
  * Dorsal venous arch of foot

---

### C. Arteriovenous Anastomosis (AV Shunt) ⭐

* Direct communication between an **artery and vein**, bypassing the capillary bed.

**Mechanism:**

* **Active organ:** AV shunts close → blood passes through capillaries.
* **Resting organ:** AV shunts open → blood bypasses capillaries and passes directly into veins.

**Features of AV shunt:**

* May be **straight or coiled**.
* Has a **thick muscular wall**.
* Controlled mainly by the **sympathetic nervous system**.

**Sites:**

* Skin of **nose, lips and external ear**
* Mucous membrane of nose and alimentary canal
* Coccygeal body
* Erectile tissue
* Tongue
* Thyroid gland
* Sympathetic ganglia

### Specialized AV anastomoses

* Present in **digital pads and nail beds**.
* Form small units called **glomera**.

### Thoroughfare channels

* Another type of shunt running through the capillary network.
* True capillaries arise as side branches.
* One thoroughfare channel + associated capillaries forms a **microcirculatory unit**.
