# Clinical Anatomy of Arches of Foot

## 1. Flat Foot — Pes Planus ⭐

**Definition:**
Absence or collapse of the **longitudinal arches** of the foot is called **flat foot (pes planus)**.

* May be **congenital or acquired**.

### Effects

1. **Loss of spring action**
   → **clumsy, shuffling gait**

2. **Loss of shock-absorbing function**
   → foot becomes more liable to **trauma and osteoarthritis**

3. **Loss of concavity of sole**
   → compression of **nerves and vessels** of the sole.

4. Compression of communication between **medial and lateral plantar nerves**
   → **neuralgic pain in forefoot = metatarsalgia**

5. Compression of blood vessels
   → **vascular disturbances in toes**

---

# 2. High-Arched Foot — Pes Cavus ⭐

**Definition:**
Exaggeration of the **longitudinal arches** of the foot is called **pes cavus (hollow foot)**.

### Cause

Usually due to **contracture (plantar flexion) at the transverse tarsal joint**.

---

## Claw Foot

When **pes cavus** is associated with:

* **Dorsiflexion of metatarsophalangeal joints**
* **Plantar flexion of interphalangeal joints**

→ the condition is called **claw foot**.

This occurs due to **atrophy of lumbricals and interossei**.

### Common causes

* **Spina bifida**
* **Poliomyelitis**

---

## 🧠 One-minute comparison

| Condition                  | Main change                                       | Important effects/associations                                        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Flat foot (pes planus)** | ↓ longitudinal arch                               | ↓ spring + ↓ shock absorption → clumsy gait, trauma, OA               |
| **Pes cavus**              | ↑ longitudinal arch                               | Usually due to plantar-flexion contracture at transverse tarsal joint |
| **Claw foot**              | Pes cavus + MTP dorsiflexion + IP plantar flexion | Atrophy of lumbricals/interossei; spina bifida, poliomyelitis         |

### ⭐ Must-remember chain

**Flat foot → loss of concavity → nerve compression → metatarsalgia**

**Pes cavus → exaggerated arch**

**Pes cavus + MTP dorsiflexion + IP plantar flexion → claw foot**


## Clinical Conditions of Foot — High Yield ⭐

### 1. Hallux valgus (Bunion) ⭐

* **Tip of great toe deviates laterally.**
* Involves the **1st metatarsophalangeal joint**.
* Cause is usually unknown; **pointed, high-heeled shoes** may contribute.
* **Bunion:** an **adventitious bursa** over the head of the 1st metatarsal.

🧠 **Valgus = big toe goes lateral**

---

### 2. Hammer toe ⭐

* Toe assumes a **hammer-like appearance**.
* **MTP joint → hyperextension**
* **PIP joint → acute flexion**
* **DIP joint → hyperextension**
* Commonly affects the **2nd and 3rd toes**.

🧠 **Hammer = PIP flexed**

---

### 3. Hallux varus

* **Great toe deviates medially**, away from the 1st metatarsal.

🧠 **Varus = big toe goes medial**

---

### 4. Hallux rigidus ⭐

* **Stiffness of the great toe**.
* Due to **degenerative arthritis and bone spur formation**.
* Affects the **1st metatarsophalangeal joint**.

🧠 **Rigidus = rigid big toe**

---

### 🔥 One-line table for revision

| Condition          | Remember                                                    |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hallux valgus**  | Great toe → **lateral**                                     |
| **Bunion**         | **Adventitious bursa** over 1st metatarsal head             |
| **Hammer toe**     | **MTP hyperextension + PIP flexion + DIP hyperextension**   |
| **Hallux varus**   | Great toe → **medial**                                      |
| **Hallux rigidus** | **Stiff great toe** due to degenerative arthritis/bone spur |

**Most important distinction:**
**Valgus = lateral** → **Varus = medial**.
