## Clinical Anatomy of Knee Joint

1. **Osteoarthritis**

   * Age-related degeneration of articular cartilage.
   * Common in elderly.
   * Presents with **gradual knee pain, stiffness and swelling**, aggravated by activity.
   * X-ray: **reduced joint space, damaged articular surfaces and osteophytes**.
   * Severe cases may require **knee replacement**.

2. **Deformities of knee**

   * **Genu valgum (knock knee):** leg is abnormally abducted.
   * **Genu varum (bow knee):** leg is abnormally adducted.
   * May occur in **rickets, abnormal posture or congenitally**.

3. **Aspiration of knee joint**

   * Joint fluid can be aspirated in case of **infection/effusion**.
   * Needle is introduced from the **lateral side**, at the midpoint of the triangle formed by:

     * Tibial tuberosity
     * Lateral epicondyle of femur
     * Apex of patella.

4. **Meniscal injuries**

   * Usually due to **twisting strain on a slightly flexed knee**.
   * **Medial meniscus is more commonly injured** because it is more firmly attached.
   * Tears may be longitudinal or transverse.
   * Longitudinal tear → **bucket-handle tear**.

5. **Cruciate ligament injuries**

   * **ACL** is more commonly injured than PCL.
   * ACL injury → violent hyperextension/anterior displacement of tibia.
   * PCL injury → posterior displacement of tibia.
   * **Anterior drawer sign:** excessive forward movement of tibia → ACL injury.
   * **Posterior drawer sign:** excessive backward movement of tibia → PCL injury.

6. **Collateral ligament injuries**

   * Less common.
   * Occur due to severe **abduction or adduction strains**.

7. **Malalignment of patella**

   * Abnormal position of patella may occur congenitally or due to tight surrounding structures.
   * May cause **painful patellofemoral conditions**.

8. **Arthroscopy**

   * Endoscopic visualization of the interior of knee joint.
   * Useful for diagnosis and **repair of damaged structures, including cruciate ligaments**.

9. **Knee replacement**

   * Done in severe joint damage.
   * Damaged articular surfaces are replaced with **artificial components**.

### ⭐ If you're very short on time

The **absolute must-know clinicals** I'd prioritize are:

**Osteoarthritis → Meniscal injury → ACL/PCL + drawer signs → Genu valgum/varum → Knee aspiration → Arthroscopy/knee replacement.**
