# 1. Gluteal Region — Overview

* Contains the **gluteal muscles** and structures connecting the pelvis with the lower limb.
* Main muscles:

  * Gluteus maximus
  * Gluteus medius
  * Gluteus minimus
  * Piriformis
  * Obturator internus
  * Superior gemellus
  * Inferior gemellus
  * Quadratus femoris
  * Obturator externus
* **Tensor fasciae latae** is also included in the region.

### Important functional points

* **Gluteus maximus**

  * Chief **extensor of hip**
  * Important in **rising from sitting**
  * Maintains erect posture
  * Antigravity muscle

* **Gluteus medius + minimus**

  * Abduct thigh
  * Maintain pelvic stability during walking
  * Prevent dropping of opposite side of pelvis

# 2. Gluteus Maximus

### Origin

* Outer slope of dorsal part of **iliac crest**
* Posterior gluteal line
* Posterior gluteal surface of ilium
* Aponeurosis of erector spinae
* Dorsal surface of lower sacrum
* Side of coccyx
* Sacrotuberous ligament

### Insertion

* **¾ → iliotibial tract**
* **¼ → gluteal tuberosity of femur**

### Nerve supply

* **Inferior gluteal nerve — L5, S1, S2**

### Actions

* Chief **extensor of hip**
* Helps in rising from sitting
* Maintains erect posture
* Lateral rotation of thigh
* Upper fibres → assist abduction
* Helps stabilise knee through iliotibial tract

### ⭐ Easy memory

**Maximus = MAX extension**

Think:

> **Maximus → maximum power for getting UP from a chair.**

# 3. Bursae Related to Gluteus Maximus

There are **3 important bursae** to remember:

| Bursa                   | Location                                            |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trochanteric bursa**  | Between gluteus maximus and greater trochanter      |
| **Ischial bursa**       | Between gluteus maximus and ischial tuberosity      |
| **Gluteofemoral bursa** | Between gluteus maximus tendon and vastus lateralis |

### Applied anatomy

* **Ischial bursitis**

  * Inflammation of ischial bursa
  * Occurs in people who sit for prolonged periods
  * Hence called **“weaver's bottom”**

### ⭐ Bursae memory

Think of gluteus maximus sitting over **3 bony/ muscular surfaces**:

**Trochanter → Ischial tuberosity → Femur**

→ **Trochanteric → Ischial → Gluteofemoral**

# 4. Greater Sciatic Foramen

### Key muscle

**Piriformis** passes through the greater sciatic foramen and divides it into:

* Structures **above piriformis**
* Structures **below piriformis**

### Above piriformis

**Superior gluteal nerve + vessels**

### Below piriformis

From **medial → lateral**:

1. Pudendal nerve
2. Internal pudendal vessels
3. Nerve to obturator internus
4. Inferior gluteal vessels
5. Inferior gluteal nerve
6. Posterior cutaneous nerve of thigh
7. Sciatic nerve
8. Nerve to quadratus femoris

### ⭐ The most important concept

**Piriformis = key muscle/gatekeeper of the greater sciatic foramen.**

So remember:

> **Above = Superior gluteal**
> **Below = basically everything else**

# 5. Lesser Sciatic Foramen

Structures passing through:

* **Tendon of obturator internus**
* **Pudendal nerve**
* **Internal pudendal vessels**
* **Nerve to obturator internus**

### Easy connection

The pudendal nerve and internal pudendal vessels:

**Greater sciatic foramen → around sacrospinous ligament → lesser sciatic foramen → perineum**

# 6. Trendelenburg Test / Sign ⭐⭐⭐

### Purpose

Tests the **abductor mechanism of the hip**, mainly:

* Gluteus medius
* Gluteus minimus

### Procedure

* Patient stands on **one leg**.
* Normally, the glutei of the weight-bearing side contract.
* They keep the opposite side of the pelvis elevated.

### Normal

Standing on **right leg**:

**Right gluteus medius/minimus contract → left side of pelvis remains elevated.**

### Positive Trendelenburg sign

If the abductors of the weight-bearing side are weak/paralysed:

→ **opposite side of pelvis drops**

Example:

**Right glutei paralysed → stand on right leg → left pelvis drops.**

### Causes

Positive sign can occur due to:

* Paralysis/weakness of gluteus medius and minimus
* Dislocation of hip
* Defective hip fulcrum
* Ununited fracture of neck of femur

# 7. Clinical / Applied Anatomy

### Testing gluteus maximus

* Patient lies **prone**.
* Patient extends hip against resistance.
* Examiner palpates gluteus maximus.

### Paralysis of gluteus maximus

* Difficulty rising from sitting.
* Patient may use hands to climb up the thighs to stand.

→ **Gower's sign**

### Gluteal injection

* Intramuscular injections are given in the **upper lateral quadrant**.
* This avoids major nerves and vessels in the lower part of the gluteal region.

### Gluteus medius/minimus paralysis

* Pelvic instability
* **Lurching gait** if unilateral
* **Waddling gait** if bilateral
* Positive **Trendelenburg sign**
