# Cardiac Dominance & Anastomoses
### Headings 
- Cardiac Dominance
- Anastomoses

## Cardiac Dominance

### Definition
- **Cardiac dominance** is determined by the artery that gives rise to the **posterior interventricular (posterior descending) artery**.

### Types

#### Right Dominance (Most common)
- **Right coronary artery (RCA)** gives the **posterior interventricular artery**.
- Hence, the **RCA is the dominant artery**.

#### Left Dominance (~10%)
- **Right coronary artery is small** and does not give the posterior interventricular artery.
- **Circumflex artery** (continuation of the left coronary artery) gives:
  - **Posterior interventricular artery**
  - Branches to the **AV node**
- Hence, the **left coronary artery is the dominant artery**.

> **The artery that gives the posterior interventricular artery is called the dominant artery.**

## Anastomosis of Coronary Arteries

### 1. Interarterial Anastomosis
- Coronary arteries are **functional end arteries**, but they do form anastomoses.
- Sites of anastomosis:
  - **Interventricular septum**
  - **Kugel's arteries** (near the **crux of the heart**)
  - **Annulus of Vieussens** (around the **pulmonary trunk**)
- Anastomoses are **better developed in older individuals**.

---

### 2. Arteriovenous Anastomosis
- Blood from a **blocked coronary artery** may flow retrogradely into:
  - **Coronary veins**
  - **Coronary sinus**
- Coronary arterial branches form **sinusoids**, which drain into the **coronary sinus**.
- Some coronary branches open **directly into the cardiac chambers**.
- **Venae cordis minimae (Thebesian veins)** open directly into the **right-sided chambers**.
- Blood within the cardiac chambers contributes to the **nutrition of the myocardium**.

---

### 3. Cardiac Anastomosis
- **Right and left coronary arteries** anastomose with each other **within the myocardium**.

---

### 4. Extracardiac Anastomosis
Coronary arteries anastomose with:

1. **Vasa vasorum of the aorta**
2. **Vasa vasorum of the pulmonary arteries**
3. **Internal thoracic arteries**
4. **Bronchial arteries**
5. **Pericardiacophrenic arteries**

> **Note:** The last three anastomose through the **pericardium**.

---

### Clinical Importance
- Extracardiac anastomoses may provide **collateral circulation** when both coronary arteries are obstructed.
- **Retrograde venous flow** may also help supply the myocardium.
- These anastomoses can act as an **alternative blood supply** in coronary artery blockage.

### Applied Anatomy
- **Coronary artery blockage (coronary thrombosis)** → **Myocardial infarction (MI)** due to ischemic death of myocardium.
