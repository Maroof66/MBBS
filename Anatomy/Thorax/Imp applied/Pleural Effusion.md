**Definition**
*   **Pleural effusion** is a clinical condition characterized by the abnormal collection of fluid in the pleural cavity, which often accompanies pleurisy (inflammation of the pleura) [1]. 

---

**Common Causes**
*   It is frequently associated with inflammation (pleuritis) [1].
*   It serves as a significant clinical sign of **tuberculosis of the lung** [2].

**Anatomical Site of Accumulation**
*   The fluid typically accumulates in the **costodiaphragmatic recess** [3]. 
*   This recess is the most dependent (lowest) part of the pleural cavity, lying inferiorly between the costal and diaphragmatic pleurae [3, 4].

---

**Clinical and Radiological Signs**
*   **Radiological finding:** On a chest radiograph, pleural effusion presents as the **obliteration of the normal costodiaphragmatic angle** [5].
*   **Mediastinal shift:** The presence of fluid can exert pressure on central structures, causing the mediastinum (and trachea) to **shift to the opposite side** of the effusion [6, 7].

---

**Clinical Management (Paracentesis Thoracis)**
*   **Procedure:** The clinical procedure to aspirate this accumulated fluid from the pleural cavity is known as **paracentesis thoracis** [1].
*   **Site of aspiration:** It is most commonly performed in the **8th intercostal space in the midaxillary line** [1].
*   **Anatomical precaution:** The aspiration needle must be inserted through the **lower part of the intercostal space** [1]. This is crucial to avoid injuring the principal neurovascular bundle (intercostal Vein, Artery, and Nerve), which courses along the upper part of the space [1].
