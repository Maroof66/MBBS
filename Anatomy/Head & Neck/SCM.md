# STERNOCLEIDOMASTOID / STERNOMASTOID

## Introduction
* long **flat strap** of muscle
* extends obliquely across the side of the neck
* **enclosed within** the **investing layer of deep cervical fascia**

## Origin
* **Sternal Head:** 
  * tendinous 
  * arises from the superolateral part of the front of the manubrium sterni
* **Clavicular Head:**
  * musculotendinous
  * arises from the medial one-­third of the superior surface of the clavicle
  * passes deep to the sternal head
* Between the two heads, there is a **small triangular depression** or the **lesser supraclavicular fossa**, **overlying the internal jugular vein**

## Insertion
1.  * By a **thick tendon** into the **lateral** surface of **mastoid process**
    * from **tip of mastoid process** to **its superior border**.
2.  * By a **thin aponeurosis** into the **lateral two-thirds** of the **superior nuchal line** of the occipital bone.

## Nerver Supply
* **Motor:** Spinal accessory nerve
* **Proprioceptive:** Ventral rami of C2 and C3

## Blood Supply
* **4 branches:**
  * 1 branch each from 
    * **superior thyroid** artery
    * **suprascapular** artery 
  * 2 branches from **occipital** artery 

## Actions 
1. When one muscle contracts:
    a. turns the chin to the opposite side.
    b. tilt the head towards the shoulder of same side.
2. When both muscles contract together:
    a. They draw the head forwards, as in eating and in lifting the head from a pillow.
    b. With the longus colli, they flex the neck against resistance.
    c. It also helps in forced inspiration.

# CLINICAL ANATOMY OF STERNOCLEIDOMASTOID

* **External Jugular Vein**

  * Pierces the fascia of the **supraclavicular space**
  * Drains into the **subclavian vein**

* **Torticollis**

  * Deformity in which:

    * **Head bends to one side**
    * **Chin points to the opposite side**

  * Due to **spasm or contracture** of muscles supplied by **spinal accessory nerve (XI)**

    * Sternocleidomastoid
    * Trapezius

  * **Types**

    * **Rheumatic torticollis**

      * Exposure to cold/draught
    * **Reflex torticollis**

      * Inflamed/suppurating cervical lymph nodes
      * Irritation of spinal accessory nerve
    * **Congenital torticollis**

      * Due to birth injury

* **Wry Neck**

  * Shortening of SCM muscle fibres
  * Due to **intravascular clotting of veins within the muscle**
  * Usually occurs following **difficult delivery**

### 🔥 Exam distinction

**Torticollis → head tilted to one side + chin to opposite side**

**Wry neck → shortening of SCM fibres, commonly after difficult delivery**
