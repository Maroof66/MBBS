# VITAMIN K 

* **Introduction**

  * Vitamin K = **“Koagulation vitamin”**.
  * Fat-soluble vitamin.
  * Essential for **blood coagulation**.

* **Chemical Nature**

  * **Naphthoquinone derivatives** with an isoprenoid side chain.
  * **Vitamin K₁ (phylloquinone):** 20-carbon side chain.
  * **Vitamin K₂:** 30-carbon side chain.
  * **Menadione:** synthetic vitamin K analogue.

* **Sources**

  * **Green leafy vegetables** — important dietary source.
  * Also synthesized by **intestinal bacteria**.

* **Absorption, Transport and Storage**

  * Absorbed from intestine along with **chylomicrons**.
  * Requires **bile salts** for normal absorption.
  * Stored mainly in the **liver**.
  * Transported in plasma with **β-lipoproteins**.

* **Biochemical Role**

  * Essential for synthesis of vitamin K-dependent coagulation factors:

    * **II — Prothrombin**
    * **VII — SPCA**
    * **IX — Christmas factor**
    * **X — Stuart-Prower factor**
  * Vitamin K acts as cofactor for **γ-carboxylation of glutamic acid residues**.
  * This forms **γ-carboxyglutamic acid (Gla)** residues.
  * Gla residues bind **Ca²⁺**, which is essential for coagulation.
  * Also required for γ-carboxylation and functional activity of **osteocalcin**.

* **Vitamin K-dependent Carboxylase**

  * Microsomal enzyme.
  * Requires:

    * O₂
    * CO₂
    * NADPH
    * Reduced vitamin K
  * **Warfarin and dicoumarol** inhibit this reaction competitively.

* **Causes of Deficiency**

  * **Fat malabsorption**

    * Obstructive jaundice
    * Chronic pancreatitis
    * Sprue
  * Prolonged **antibiotic therapy**
  * Gastrointestinal infections with diarrhoea
  * Newborns, especially **premature infants**, are at risk.

* **Clinical Manifestations of Deficiency**

  * **Bleeding tendency**
  * Bruising and ecchymotic patches
  * Mucosal haemorrhage
  * Post-traumatic/internal bleeding
  * **Prolonged prothrombin time (PT)**
  * Delayed clotting
  * **Haemorrhagic disease of newborn**

* **Clinical Importance**

  * **Warfarin and dicoumarol** act as anticoagulants by inhibiting vitamin K-dependent γ-carboxylation.
  * PT is useful in assessing **liver function** and coagulation status.

* **RDA**

  * Approximately **50–100 µg/day** *(check the unit: it is µg, not mg)*.

* **Hypervitaminosis**

  * Excessive synthetic vitamin K/menadione may cause:

    * Haemolysis
    * Hyperbilirubinaemia
    * Kernicterus
    * Brain damage

### 🔥 Absolute must-remember for the 5-marker

**Vitamin K → γ-carboxylation → Gla residues → Ca²⁺ binding → Factors II, VII, IX, X**

And the classic clinical connection:

**Vitamin K deficiency → ↑ PT → bleeding**

**Warfarin → inhibits vitamin K cycle → anticoagulant**
