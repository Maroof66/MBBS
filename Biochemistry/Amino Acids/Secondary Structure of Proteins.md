# Secondary Structure of Proteins

### Headings

- Definition

---

## 1. Definition

- Secondary structure = configurational relationship between amino acid residues about **3–4 residues apart** in the linear sequence.
- Stabilized mainly by **noncovalent forces**.

## 2. Stabilizing Forces

- Hydrogen bonds
- Electrostatic (ionic) bonds
- Hydrophobic interactions
- van der Waals forces

> **High-yield:** van der Waals forces are individually weak but collectively contribute maximally to protein stability.

---

## 3. α-Helix

- Most common and stable conformation of a polypeptide chain.
- **Spiral structure** with side chains projecting outward.
- Stabilized by **hydrogen bonds between NH and C=O groups** of the same polypeptide chain.
- **3.6 amino acid residues/turn**.
- Rise = **1.5 Å per residue**.
- Usually **right-handed**.
- **Proline and hydroxyproline disrupt/prevent α-helix formation**.
- Abundant in **hemoglobin and myoglobin**.

### Key numbers
**α-helix → 3.6 residues/turn → 1.5 Å/residue**

---

## 4. β-Pleated Sheet

- Polypeptide chain is **almost fully extended**.
- Distance between adjacent amino acid residues = **3.5 Å**.
- Stabilized by **hydrogen bonds between NH and C=O groups of neighboring polypeptide segments**.
- Two types:
  - **Parallel** → chains run in the same direction.
  - **Antiparallel** → chains run in opposite directions.
- Examples:
  - Silk fibroin → mainly **antiparallel**
  - Flavodoxin → **parallel**
  - Carbonic anhydrase → **both**

### β-bends
- Formed by an abrupt **U-turn** of the polypeptide chain.
- Intrachain disulfide bridges may stabilize the bend.

---

## 5. Collagen Helix

- **Triple-helical structure** found in collagen.
- Three polypeptide chains wind around each other to form a **rod-like superhelix**.
- **3.3 amino acid residues/turn**.
- Distance between turns = **2.9 Å**.
- Three chains are stabilized by **hydrogen bonds**.
- **Glycine** is essential because its small size allows it to fit into the crowded interior.

### Special point
- **Type IV collagen** → triple helices are discontinuous → forms a **mesh-like network** in basement membranes.

### Gelatin
- Boiling collagen disrupts its native triple-helical structure.
- It becomes a **random coil** and is called **gelatin**.
