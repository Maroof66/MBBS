
# Recombinant DNA Technology 

* **1. Preparation of Specific Gene**

  * Specific mRNA is isolated from the tissue expressing the required gene.
  * mRNA is converted into **cDNA by reverse transcriptase**.

* **2. Preparation of Recombinant DNA**

  * **Plasmid vector** and foreign DNA are cut by the **same restriction endonuclease**.
  * Complementary **sticky ends** are produced.
  * Foreign DNA is annealed with the plasmid.
  * **DNA ligase** joins them by phosphodiester bonds.
  * → **Recombinant/chimeric DNA** is formed.

* **3. Introduction into Host and Cloning**

  * Recombinant plasmid is introduced into **E. coli** (**transfection/transformation**).
  * Host cells multiply and produce multiple copies of recombinant DNA.
  * This is called **cloning**.

## Role of Vector

* **Vector = carrier of foreign DNA into host cell.**
* Commonly used vector: **plasmid**.
* Important functions:

  * Carries the foreign DNA.
  * Replicates inside the host.
  * Contains **selectable marker genes**, commonly antibiotic-resistance genes.
  * May act as an **expression vector** for production of desired protein.

## Plasmid-Based Antibiotic-Resistance Selection

* Example: **pBR-325 plasmid** contains:

  * **Ampᴿ** → ampicillin resistance
  * **Tetᴿ** → tetracycline resistance
  * **Cmᴿ** → chloramphenicol resistance

* **EcoRI** cuts within the **Cmᴿ gene**.

* Insertion of foreign DNA → **inactivation of Cmᴿ gene**.

### Selection

**After transfection:**

**Ampicillin + Tetracycline plate**
→ Only bacteria containing plasmid survive.

↓

**Replica plating on chloramphenicol**
→ Non-recombinant bacteria (**Cmᴿ**) survive.

→ Recombinant bacteria (**Cmˢ**) die.

↓

**Colonies present on first plate but absent on chloramphenicol plate**
→ **Desired recombinant colonies**

↓

**Cloning → Expression → Desired protein**

### 🔥 The one flowchart I'd memorize

**Gene/mRNA → cDNA → Restriction enzyme → Sticky ends → Annealing → DNA ligase → Recombinant plasmid → E. coli → Antibiotic selection → Recombinant colony → Cloning → Expression**
