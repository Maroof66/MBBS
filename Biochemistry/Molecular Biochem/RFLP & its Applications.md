# RFLP and Applications

* **1. Definition**

  * **Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (RFLP)** is the existence of **two or more restriction-fragment patterns** due to variation in DNA base sequences.
  * It can be used as a **genetic marker**.

* **2. Basis / Principle**

  * Human DNA contains naturally occurring variations called **polymorphisms**.
  * Polymorphism is generally present in **>1% of the population** and usually has no harmful effect.
  * Variation in DNA sequence may alter the site of a **restriction enzyme**, producing fragments of different lengths.

* **3. Procedure**

  * DNA is treated with a **restriction endonuclease**.
  * DNA is cleaved into fragments of different lengths.
  * Fragments are separated by **agarose gel electrophoresis**.
  * DNA is transferred to nitrocellulose membrane by **Southern blotting**.
  * Fragments are hybridized with a **labelled DNA probe**.
  * → Altered restriction-fragment pattern indicates **RFLP**.

### Applications

* **1. Disputed parenthood**

  * RFLP markers can be used to trace chromosomes from **parent to offspring**.

* **2. Human population genetics**

  * Study of genetic variation among populations.
  * Useful in studying **geographical isolates**.

* **3. Comparison of related species**

  * Helps compare the **genetic makeup of related species**.

* **4. Diagnosis of genetic diseases**

  * Genetic diseases may alter the size/distribution of restriction fragments.
  * These changes can be detected as **RFLP patterns**.

### 🔥 Remember the procedure

**DNA → Restriction enzyme → DNA fragments → Agarose electrophoresis → Southern blotting → Labelled probe → RFLP pattern**
