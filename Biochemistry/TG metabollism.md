
TRIACYLGLYCEROL METABOLISM AND ITS REGULATION

Long Answer Question – 10 Marks

1. Introduction

- Triacylglycerol (TAG) or triglyceride is the major storage form of fat in the body.
- It consists of glycerol esterified with three fatty acids.
- Major sites of TAG metabolism:
  - Intestine – digestion, absorption and resynthesis
  - Liver – synthesis and secretion as VLDL
  - Adipose tissue – storage and mobilization

4. Transport of TAG

A. Chylomicrons

- Synthesized in the intestinal mucosa.
- Transport dietary TAG from intestine to peripheral tissues.
- TAG is hydrolysed by LPL.
- Chylomicron remnants are taken up by the liver.

B. VLDL

- Synthesized in the liver.
- Transports endogenous TAG from liver to peripheral tissues.
- TAG is hydrolysed by LPL.

7. Clinical Significance

- Excessive TAG accumulation is associated with obesity and metabolic disorders.
- Increased plasma TAG causes hypertriglyceridemia.
- Severe hypertriglyceridemia can predispose to acute pancreatitis.
- Abnormal TAG metabolism contributes to cardiovascular disease risk.

---

Conclusion

- TAG is the major energy storage form of fat.
- It is synthesized mainly in the liver, adipose tissue and intestine.
- TAG is transported mainly by chylomicrons and VLDL.
- During fasting, TAG undergoes lipolysis, releasing fatty acids for energy production.
