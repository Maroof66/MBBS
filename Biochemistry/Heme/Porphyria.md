## 1. Definition

* **Porphyrias** are a group of **inborn errors of heme biosynthesis**.
* They are characterized by increased production and excretion of **porphyrins and/or their precursors (ALA and PBG)**.
* Most are inherited as **autosomal dominant** disorders.

---

# 2. Classification

### Based on the major site of enzyme defect:

1. **Hepatic porphyrias**
2. **Erythropoietic porphyrias**
3. **Mixed hepatic + erythropoietic porphyrias**

### Clinically, remember two major patterns:

* 🧠 **Neurovisceral** → abdominal pain + neurological/psychiatric symptoms
* ☀️ **Cutaneous** → photosensitivity + skin lesions

---

# 3. Acute Intermittent Porphyria (AIP) ⭐⭐⭐

### Cause

* **Autosomal dominant**
* Deficiency of **PBG deaminase (uroporphyrinogen-I-synthase)**
* ↓ PBG deaminase → ↑ **ALA + PBG**

### Clinical features

**Mainly neurovisceral — NO photosensitivity**

* Acute **abdominal pain** — most common
* Nausea/vomiting may occur
* **Neurological manifestations:**

  * Sensory disturbances
  * Motor weakness
  * Neuropathy
* **Psychiatric manifestations:**

  * Confusion
  * Agitation
  * Psychiatric symptoms

### Important points

* Symptoms occur **intermittently**.
* Attacks may be precipitated by:

  * **Starvation**
  * Porphyrinogenic drugs, e.g. **barbiturates**
* Symptoms improve with **high-carbohydrate intake**.
* **No photosensitivity** because porphyrins are not increased.

### Diagnosis

* ↑ **ALA and PBG** in blood and urine.
* Urine may become **dark/red on standing** due to oxidation of PBG.
* **Ehrlich's reagent** detects porphyrin precursors.
* Fresh urine should be protected from light for PBG estimation.

### Treatment

* Remove precipitating factors/drugs.
* **High carbohydrate/glucose** during acute attacks.
* **Heme/hemin therapy** in significant acute attacks.

### 🧠 AIP = **Abdominal + ALA/PBG + Psychiatric + no Photosensitivity**

---

# 4. Congenital Erythropoietic Porphyria ⭐⭐

### Cause

* **Autosomal recessive**
* Defect in **uroporphyrinogen III synthase**
* Excess formation of **type I porphyrins**

### Clinical features

Mainly **cutaneous/photosensitivity**:

* Severe **photosensitivity**
* Recurrent dermatitis
* Skin ulceration and scarring
* Facial deformity may develop
* **Erythrodontia** → teeth show **red fluorescence under UV light**
* Urine may appear **dark red / port-wine coloured**

### Diagnosis

* Increased porphyrins in blood and urine
* **UV fluorescence** is useful.

### Treatment

* Avoid sunlight and use strict **photoprotection**.
* Specialist treatment may include **red-cell suppression/transfusion strategies** or **hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation** in severe disease.

### 🧠 Remember

**Congenital erythropoietic → CHILD + SUN + RED**

---

# 5. Erythropoietic Protoporphyria (EPP)

### Cause

* **Ferrochelatase deficiency**
* ↓ Ferrochelatase → ↑ **free protoporphyrin in RBCs**

### Clinical features

* Mainly **photosensitivity**
* Burning sensation and inflammatory reaction on sun exposure
* May develop cutaneous lesions
* Usually due to accumulation of **lipophilic protoporphyrin** in cell membranes.

### Diagnosis

* ↑ free protoporphyrin in RBCs
* ↓ ferrochelatase activity
* RBCs show **red fluorescence at 620 nm**

### Treatment

* **Sun protection**
* Avoidance of excessive sunlight
* Specialist therapy may include **afamelanotide** in appropriate patients.

---

# 6. Other Important Porphyrias

### ALA dehydratase deficiency porphyria

* Very rare.
* Deficiency of **ALA dehydratase (porphobilinogen synthase)**.
* Features resemble AIP:

  * **Abdominal pain**
  * **Neuropathy**
* ↑ **ALA** in plasma and urine.
* Differential diagnosis:

  * **Lead poisoning**
  * Tyrosinemia type I

### Acquired porphyria — Lead poisoning

* Lead inhibits:

  * **ALA dehydratase**
  * **Ferrochelatase**
* ↓ heme → ↑ ALA synthase activity.
* Important distinction:

> **Acquired porphyria → associated anemia**, unlike most congenital porphyrias.

---

# 7. Diagnosis of Porphyrias ⭐⭐⭐

### Screening / detection

* **Urinary ALA and PBG** → important in acute neurovisceral porphyrias.
* **Porphyrins in urine, blood and feces** depending on the type.
* **Ehrlich's reagent** → detects porphyrin precursors.
* **UV fluorescence** → porphyrins show characteristic **red fluorescence**.
* Specific enzyme assay/genetic testing may confirm the diagnosis.

### Key principle

> **Neurovisceral symptoms → look for ALA + PBG**
> **Photosensitivity → look for porphyrins**

---

# 8. Treatment — General Principles

1. **Avoid precipitating factors**

   * Porphyrinogenic drugs
   * Starvation
   * Excess alcohol where relevant

2. **Acute neurovisceral attacks**

   * High carbohydrate/glucose
   * **Heme/hemin therapy** for significant attacks

3. **Cutaneous porphyrias**

   * Strict **sun protection**
   * Treat the specific porphyria appropriately.
