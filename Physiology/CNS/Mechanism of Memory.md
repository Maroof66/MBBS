### 1. Definition / Basic Concept

* Learning and memory are established by **long-lasting changes in synaptic function and neuronal connections**.
* Important neurochemical changes include:

  * ↑ Neurotransmitter release
  * Formation of **intracellular second messengers**
  * **Protein phosphorylation**
  * **Protein synthesis**
  * **Gene activation**
  * Formation/strengthening of **synaptic connections**

---

**Protein phosphorylation**

**Protein synthesis & gene activation**
↓
**Strengthening / formation of synaptic connections**
↓
**Learning & memory**

### 3. Long-Term Potentiation (LTP) ⭐

* **LTP** = long-lasting increase in **synaptic strength** following repeated/high-frequency stimulation.
* It is an important mechanism for **learning and memory**.
* Mainly studied in the **hippocampus**.

**Repeated / high-frequency stimulation**
↓
**↑ Intracellular Ca²⁺**
↓
**Protein phosphorylation**
↓
**↑ Neuronal excitability & synaptic strength**
↓
**Protein synthesis**
↓
**Formation/strengthening of synaptic connections**
↓
**Long-term memory**

### 5. Habituation

* Repeated application of a **neutral stimulus** → progressively **decreased response**.
* Mechanism:

**Repeated stimulus**
↓
**↓ Serotonin**
↓
**↓ Ca²⁺ entry into presynaptic terminal**
↓
**↓ Neurotransmitter release**
↓
**Decreased response**

---

### 6. Sensitization

* Repeated/noisome stimulus → **increased response**.
* It occurs by **presynaptic facilitation**.

**Noxious stimulus**
↓
**↑ Serotonin**
↓
**↑ cAMP**
↓
**K⁺ channel closure**
↓
**Prolonged action potential**
↓
**↑ Ca²⁺ influx**
↓
**↑ Neurotransmitter release**
↓
**Increased response**

---

### 7. Classical Conditioning

* Pairing of **UCS with CS** produces biochemical changes in target neurons.
* Basic mechanism involves **presynaptic facilitation**.

**UCS + CS**
↓
**↑ Ca²⁺ in presynaptic terminal**
↓
**↑ Adenylyl cyclase activity**
↓
**↑ cAMP**
↓
**K⁺ channel phosphorylation & closure**
↓
**Prolonged action potential**
↓
**Presynaptic facilitation**

---

### 8. Post-tetanic Potentiation

* **Post-tetanic potentiation** = enhanced postsynaptic potentials following a brief high-frequency (**tetanizing**) stimulation.
* Tetanizing stimulation → **Ca²⁺ accumulation in presynaptic neuron** → enhanced neurotransmitter release.
* Effect lasts for **up to about 60 seconds**.

---

### 9. Declarative and Procedural Memory

**Declarative memory**

* Memory of **facts and events**.
* Mainly involves **medial temporal lobe**.

**Procedural memory**

* Memory of **skills and habits**.
* Involves:

  * Striatum
  * Motor cortex
  * Cerebellum

---

### 10. Role of Cholinergic Neurons

* **Acetylcholine** is an important neurotransmitter in learning and memory.
* Cholinergic neurons in the **basal forebrain** project to:

  * Hippocampus
  * Cerebral cortex
* Loss of cortical/subcortical cholinergic neurons is associated with **memory impairment and dementia**.
