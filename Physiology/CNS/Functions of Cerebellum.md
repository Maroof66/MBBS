# FUNCTIONS OF CEREBELLUM

## Functional Division → Main Function

| Region | Main Function |
|---|---|
| **Vestibulocerebellum** | Balance + eye movements |
| **Spinocerebellum** | Ongoing movement + posture + muscle tone |
| **Neocerebellum** | Planning + programming + skilled movement + motor learning |

---

# 1. VESTIBULOCEREBELLUM

### A. Balance & Equilibrium
- Maintains **postural balance and equilibrium**.
- Has extensive reciprocal connections with **vestibular nuclei**.
- Receives vestibular inputs from the **inner ear** via vestibular nuclei.

### B. Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex (VOR)
- Helps in **learning-induced modification of VOR**.
- Maintains appropriate eye position during head movement.

### C. Vestibular Functions
- Balance during movement.
- Vestibular postural reflexes.
- Adjustment of posture and movement with:
  - Head movement
  - Acceleration

### D. Eye Movements
- **Paraflocculus and pyramis** are involved in eyeball movements.
- Particularly involved in **upward eye movement**.
- Contributes to **visual judgment of distance**.

---

# 2. SPINOCEREBELLUM

## A. Smoothening & Coordination of Movement

### Inputs

```text
Proprioceptive inputs
        +
Sensory inputs from body
        +
Motor plan from motor cortex
        ↓
   SPINOCEREBELLUM
        ↓
Compare "planned" vs "actual" movement
        ↓
Correct errors
        ↓
Smooth + coordinated movement
```

### Therefore
- Coordinates **ongoing movements**.
- Controls:
  - Rate
  - Range
  - Force
  - Direction
  - Termination of movement.

---

## B. Vermal Spinocerebellum → Posture

```text
VERMIS
  ↓
Brainstem nuclei
  ↓
Axial + proximal limb muscles
  ↓
POSTURE
```

- Controls **posture**.
- Particularly influences **axial and proximal muscles**.

---

## C. Paravermal Spinocerebellum → Skilled Movements

```text
PARAVERMIS
    ↓
Brainstem motor nuclei
    ↓
Distal limb muscles
    ↓
Skilled voluntary movements
```

- Controls **skilled voluntary movements** of limbs.

---

## D. Muscle Tone

- Cerebellum influences **descending medial motor pathways** through the **fastigial nucleus**.
- Mainly:
  - Vestibulospinal tract → **α motor neurons**
  - Reticulospinal tract → **γ motor neurons**
- Thus, cerebellum contributes to **α–γ co-linkage**.

### Clinical

```text
Cerebellar lesion
      ↓
↓ Excitatory output to brainstem motor nuclei
      ↓
↓ Muscle tone
      ↓
HYPOTONIA
```

- **Stretch reflexes are usually normal**.
- Occasionally → **pendular knee jerk**.

---

# 3. NEOCEREBELLUM

## A. Planning & Programming of Movement

- Interacts extensively with the **cerebral cortex**.
- Responsible for:
  - Planning of movements
  - Programming of movements
  - Planning of **sequential movements**
  - Timing of sequential movements

```text
Neocerebellum
      ↕
Motor / Premotor cortex
      ↓
Planning + Programming
      ↓
Sequential + Timed movements
```

---

## B. Skilled Voluntary Movement

- Cerebellar hemisphere helps coordinate **skilled voluntary movements**.
- Particularly important for **timing and precision**.

---

# 4. CEREBELLUM AS A "COMPARATOR"

### Basic Concept

```text
        MOTOR PLAN
     (Cerebral cortex)
             │
             ▼
       Intended movement
             │
             ▼
        ┌───────────┐
        │ CEREBELLUM│
        │ COMPARATOR│
        └───────────┘
             ▲
             │
       Actual movement
             │
     Proprioceptive input
             │
             ▼
        Muscle + joint
```

### Cerebellum receives
- Motor commands from **cerebral cortex**.
- Proprioceptive information through **spinocerebellar tracts**.
- Special sensory information:
  - Visual
  - Auditory
  - Vestibular

### Then

```text
Planned movement
       +
Actual movement
       ↓
Cerebellum compares
       ↓
Detects error
       ↓
Corrective signal
       ↓
Motor cortex / descending pathways
       ↓
Modified movement
```

> **Therefore:** Cerebellum acts as a **comparator / servo mechanism**.

---

# 5. PREVENTS OVERSHOOT & TREMOR

```text
Fast movement
     ↓
Tendency to overshoot
     ↓
Cerebellar damping
     ↓
Prevents overshoot
     ↓
Smooth movement
```

- Prevents **overshoot**.
- Prevents excessive oscillation/tremor.
- Acts as a **damping system**.

---

# 6. MOTOR LEARNING

- Cerebellum compares:
  - **Ongoing movement**
  - **Required movement**
- Generates corrective signals → improves performance.

### Long-term role
- Learning of **motor skills**.
- Long-term adjustment of motor performance.

### Important pathway

```text
Climbing fibres
      ↓
Purkinje cells
      ↓
Complex spikes
      ↓
Motor learning
```

- Climbing fibre activity increases during learning of a **new activity**.

---

# 7. CONTROL OF IPSILATERAL MOVEMENTS

### Why does each cerebellar hemisphere control the same side?

```text
Cerebral cortex
      ↓
Corticopontine fibres
      ↓
Pontine nuclei
      ↓
Cross
      ↓
Contralateral cerebellar hemisphere
      ↓
Deep cerebellar nuclei
      ↓
Cross again
      ↓
Same cerebral hemisphere
      ↓
Corticospinal tract
      ↓
Crosses at pyramidal decussation
      ↓
Original side of body
```

### Result

> **Each cerebellar hemisphere controls coordination of movements on the ipsilateral side of the body.**

**Reason → DOUBLE DECUSSATION.**
