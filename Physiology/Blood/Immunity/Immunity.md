# IMMUNITY

* **Definition:** Immunity is defined as the capacity of the body to resist pathogenic agents.
* It is the **ability of body to resist the entry** of different types of foreign bodies *(bacteria, virus)*.

* **Immunity is of 2 types :—**
  * a. Innate Immunity
  * b. Acquired Immunity

---

## 1. INNATE IMMUNITY (NON-SPECIFIC IMMUNITY)

* The **inborn capacity of body** to resist pathogens.
* Otherwise called **natural / non-specific immunity**.
* Represents the **1ˢᵗ line of defense**.

### Mechanisms of Innate Immunity

| Structures and Mediators | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal tract** | • Enzymes in digestive juices and the acid in stomach destroy the toxic substances or organisms entering digestive tract through food.<br>• Lysozyme present in saliva destroys bacteria. |
| **Respiratory system** | • Defensins and cathelicidins in epithelial cells of air passage are antimicrobial peptides.<br>• Neutrophils, lymphocytes, macrophages and natural killer cells present in lungs act against bacteria and virus. |
| **Urinogenital system** | • Acidity in urine and vaginal fluid destroy the bacteria. |
| **Skin** | • The keratinized stratum corneum of epidermis protects the skin against toxic chemicals.<br>• The $\beta$-defensins in skin are antimicrobial peptides.<br>• Lysozyme secreted in skin destroys bacteria. |
| **Phagocytic cells** | • Neutrophils, monocytes and macrophages ingest and destroy the microorganisms and foreign bodies by phagocytosis. |
| **Interferons** | • Inhibit multiplication of viruses, parasites and cancer cells. |
| **Complement proteins** | • Accelerate the destruction of microorganisms. |

---

## 2. ACQUIRED IMMUNITY (SPECIFIC IMMUNITY)

* **Definition:** The resistance developed in the body against any specific foreign body like bacteria, viruses, toxins, vaccines.
* **Most powerful immune mechanism** *(Lymphocytes)*.

* **2 Types of Acquired Immunity :—**
  * a. **Cellular Immunity** $\longrightarrow$ Development of cellular immunity by **T-lymphocytes / T-cells**.
  * b. **Humoral Immunity** $\longrightarrow$ Development of humoral immunity by **B-lymphocytes / B-cells**.

---

### T-LYMPHOCYTES

* **Processed in:** Thymus *(secretes hormone thymosin)*.
* **Types :—**
  * a. Helper T-cells / Inducer T-cells ($\text{CD}_4\text{ cells}$)
  * b. Cytotoxic / Killer T-cells ($\text{CD}_8\text{ cells}$)
  * c. Suppressor T-cells
  * d. Memory T-cells
* **Storage:** After transformation, all T-lymphocytes stored in lymphoid tissues of lymph node, spleen, bone marrow & GI tract.

---

### B-LYMPHOCYTES

* **Processed in:**
  * Liver *(during fetal life)*
  * Bone marrow *(after birth)*
* **Types :—**
  * a. Plasma cells
  * b. Memory cells
* **Storage:** After transformation, stored in lymphoid tissue of lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow.

---

### ANTIGENS

* **Definition:** Antigens are substances which induce specific immune reactions in body.

* **2 Types :—**
  * a. **Autoantigens / Self Antigens:** Present on body's own cells *('A' antigen, 'B' antigen)*.
  * b. **Foreign Antigens / Non-Self Antigens:** Enter body from outside.
