# HEART SOUNDS

* **Definition :** Sounds produced by the mechanical activities of the heart during each cardiac cycle.
* **Causes of Production :—**
  * 1. Flow of blood through cardiac chambers
  * 2. Contraction of cardiac muscle
  * 3. Closure of valves of the heart
* **Auscultation & Recording :** Heard using a **stethoscope** or **microphone**; graphical registration is studied via **phonocardiogram**.
* **Clinical Diagnostic Value :** Alterations in heart sounds indicate underlying cardiac pathologies *(especially valvular heart diseases)*.

---

## CLASSIFICATION OF HEART SOUNDS

* **1ˢᵗ & 2ⁿᵈ Heart Sounds :—**
  * Termed **classical heart sounds**.
  * Resemble the spoken words **'LUB' (LUBB)** and **'DUB' (DUP)** respectively.
* **3ʳᵈ Heart Sound :—**
  * Mild sound; normally **not heard with a regular stethoscope**, but detectable using a sensitive microphone/phonocardiogram.
* **4ᵗʰ Heart Sound :—**
  * Inaudible physiological sound; **becomes audible only under pathological conditions**.
  * Studied primarily by phonocardiography.

## APPLIED PHYSIOLOGY OF HEART SOUNDS

* **1. Reduplication / Splitting of Heart Sounds :—**
  * Means splitting of normal heart sounds.
  * **S₁ Splitting :** Occurs when atrioventricular valves (mitral and tricuspid) do not close simultaneously.
  * **S₂ Splitting :** Occurs when semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonary) do not close simultaneously.

* **2. Soft Heart Sounds :—**
  * Heart sounds become soft when intensity is $\downarrow$ decreased.
  * Characteristically heard in **low blood pressure (hypotension)** and shock.

* **3. Loud Heart Sounds :—**
  * Heart sounds produced with much higher intensity than normal *(e.g., hyperdynamic states, hypertension, valvular stenosis)*.
